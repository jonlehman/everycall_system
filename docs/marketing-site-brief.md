# EveryCall Website Marketing Brief

## Purpose
This document is the source brief for any marketing AI, designer, copywriter, or developer building the EveryCall website.

It explains what EveryCall does today, who it is for, why customers would buy it, how to describe the product from the client's perspective, and what claims must be avoided.

This brief is based on the current codebase and product documentation, not aspirational roadmap language.

## One-Sentence Description
EveryCall is an AI sales receptionist for service businesses that answers inbound calls, responds using the business's real information, captures qualified leads, and gives the team a clean next step through a client workspace, alerts, integrations, and optional blind transfers to configured team members.

## Short Product Summary
EveryCall helps service businesses stop losing inbound opportunities when they cannot answer the phone.

When a call comes in, EveryCall answers quickly, speaks in a warm and natural tone, understands what the caller needs, answers business-specific questions using approved knowledge, collects the right contact and job details, and routes the outcome into the business's workflow. When the caller specifically asks for a configured team member, EveryCall can also blind-transfer the call by name or extension.

For the client, the value is not just "AI answering calls." The real value is:
- fewer missed opportunities
- better first impression for callers
- more complete lead capture
- less time spent chasing incomplete voicemails
- better visibility into what callers asked and what happened
- an easier way to improve answers over time

## Core End-User Story
From the client's perspective, EveryCall should be described like this:

"EveryCall gives your business an AI sales receptionist that answers every inbound call, talks to customers like a capable front desk assistant, uses your real business information to answer questions, collects the details your team needs, and hands off the next step cleanly."

This is stronger and more accurate than describing EveryCall as a generic AI phone bot.

## Primary Customer
The best-fit customer today is a service business that gets valuable inbound calls and cannot always answer them live.

Examples:
- plumbing
- HVAC
- electrical
- roofing
- window installation

- landscaping

- cleaning
- locksmith
- general contractors

The broader product also supports other business types through its knowledge system and domain model, including medical, dental, therapy, legal, accounting, education, wellness, and professional services. But the clearest commercial story right now is service businesses with missed-call pain and high-value inbound leads.

## Primary User Jobs To Be Done
Clients hire EveryCall to:
- answer calls when nobody on the team can pick up
- handle after-hours and overflow calls professionally
- make sure new leads do not go to voicemail
- collect caller details in a usable way
- answer common business questions without guessing
- let callers reach configured team members by name or extension when appropriate
- send the team a clean summary and next step
- reduce the chaos of missed calls, bad notes, and incomplete messages

## Main Problems The Website Should Speak To
The site should speak directly to these problems:

### 1. Missed Calls Become Lost Revenue
When the team is on-site, driving, with a customer, or closed for the day, important calls get missed.

### 2. Voicemails Are Incomplete
Callers leave partial details, do not explain the real issue clearly, or never leave a message at all.

### 3. Staff Time Is Expensive
Owners and dispatchers should not spend their day screening every call or reconstructing what happened from scraps.

### 4. Inconsistent Answers Hurt Trust
If callers ask about service area, hours, financing, warranty, or policies, a weak answer creates doubt and lowers conversion.

### 5. Teams Need Follow-Up, Not More Noise
The business needs the outcome of the call in a clean format, not another messy voicemail to interpret.

## How EveryCall Works
Use this simple client-facing explanation:

1. Your inbound calls reach EveryCall.
2. EveryCall answers immediately with your business identity and voice.
3. It listens to what the caller needs and keeps the conversation short and clear.
4. It answers business questions using your approved website content, uploaded documents, and configured rules.
5. It captures the caller's information and job details.
6. It gives your team the result through the workspace, alerts, integrations, or a blind transfer when the caller explicitly asks for a configured person or extension.
7. You can review calls, improve knowledge, and refine how the receptionist behaves over time.

## Critical Claim Guardrails
These are important. The website should not overstate what exists today.

### Do Not Claim Calendar Booking As A Core Live Capability
Do not say:
- books appointments automatically
- confirms jobs on the calendar
- dispatches technicians automatically

Safer language:
- moves callers to the right next step
- captures qualified leads, transfer requests, and next-step details
- helps your team follow up quickly

### Do Not Claim Fully Automated Dispatch
Current product language and flow support routing and follow-up, not true end-to-end automated dispatch.

### Do Not Claim It Gives Technical Advice
The product is intentionally designed not to diagnose or provide technical advice.

### Do Not Claim The System Knows Everything Automatically
Its strength is approved business knowledge, not unlimited factual certainty.

### Do Not Promise Supported Integrations Beyond What Exists In The Client App
Current visible integration surfaces support:
- Zapier
- HubSpot
- Jobber
- ServiceTitan

If more are mentioned, they should be clearly marked as roadmap or omitted.

## Claims That Need Extra Care
These areas are close enough to roadmap language that marketing AI should be conservative:

- Booking:
  talk about lead capture, callback, transfer, and next-step progression unless true booking workflow is being shown and verified.
- Dispatch:
  do not describe EveryCall as automated dispatch software.
- Technical advice:
  do not describe the receptionist as diagnosing problems or giving expert repair guidance.
- Vertical breadth:
  the product supports more than home services, but home-service positioning is the clearest public story today.
- Integrations:
  only name integrations that are present in the client app and current implementation.
